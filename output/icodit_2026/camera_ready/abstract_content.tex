A controller that keeps learning after its sensing changes should improve under the new condition without losing what it could already do, so both outcomes must be measured. We study continued Soft Actor-Critic training of a history-conditioned transformer and a reactive controller after the onset of observation noise or a three-step observation delay in HalfCheetah-v5 and Ant-v5. Eight source checkpoints per controller and task each branch into the two faults, with or without half of each batch drawn from earlier clean experience, plus a control that continues on clean sensing. Every branch runs 20,000 steps, and the same weights are evaluated under the fault and under clean sensing. In HalfCheetah, the reactive controller improved under delay (\R{hc-reactive-delay-0-dB}) while its clean return fell (\R{hc-reactive-delay-0-dA}); the transformer did not improve under delay and lost clean return (\R{hc-transformer-delay-0-dA}). There, retained replay reduced each clean-condition loss, with all eight seeds agreeing, but shrank the reactive controller's delay gain, and an exploratory critic diagnostic shows that under delay, mixing kept the critics' values on delayed histories inflated relative to clean ones. The Ant sources learned to stand rather than walk, and the faults cost them little. Inference took \R{lat-transformer-cpu1-mean}\,ms per action for the transformer on one CPU thread. The study reproduces an earlier three-seed pilot exactly but does not confirm its small transformer gain under delay.
